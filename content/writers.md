This page is for independent writers, small presses and publications who want a website of their own. That might be a home for the books or the essays, an archive that keeps everything published so far in order, or a way off a hosted platform that does not lose what is already there.

## What you get

- **A website that is yours.** The domain is registered in your name. The writing, the images and the files that make the site are handed over in full, so the site can move to another host without anyone's permission, suteo's included.
- **Writing kept apart from design.** Each piece is a plain text file in Markdown, a simple format that reads like an ordinary document. A new essay can be added without touching the design, and the design can change later without touching the essays.
- **An archive that stays in order.** Published work is arranged the way readers look for it: by book, by series, by form or by subject. Every piece keeps a fixed address, so links to it keep working.
- **Quiet pages.** No analytics, no tracking pixels, no pop-ups and no fonts fetched from someone else's server. Light and dark modes and text-size controls, as on every site in the house.
- **A feed for readers who want one.** New pieces appear in an RSS feed, which lets readers follow the site in the reading app they already use.
- **Nothing to keep patched.** The finished site is a set of ready-made pages. There is no database and no admin login, which keeps it quick to load and simple to host.

## The work so far

suteo has built, and maintains, six sites for [nox](/work/nox/), an independent publishing house:

- [nox](/work/nox/), the house catalogue, which updates itself when one of the names publishes.
- [iacula](/work/iacula/), an author site that keeps in-world histories apart from the books in the source and shows them together on the page.
- [lux](/work/lux/), a notebook whose five forms of writing each have a section of their own, rather than sharing one stream of posts.
- [lorica](/work/lorica/), long essays first, with plain tags for following one thread across the archive.
- [yod](/work/yod/), the most reduced of the six: one typeface, very little but the text, and every post offered as a feed.
- [selador](/work/selador/), a gallery whose sales are handled by Ko-fi, so the site itself carries no shop.

The moves so far have been the practice's own. This journal began on another host and under another name. When it moved, the three original essays kept their text, their dates and their addresses, and links to them kept working. No client has yet been moved off a hosted platform, so there is no outside example to show here.

## Moving off a hosted platform

Substack, Ghost, Medium, WordPress.com, Squarespace and similar services make it easy to start and harder to leave. A move covers:

- every published piece, with its title, date and images;
- the old addresses, redirected (sent on automatically) to the new ones wherever the old platform allows it, so links in other people's writing still arrive somewhere;
- the subscriber list, where the platform lets you export it.

What each platform lets you take out differs, and some of it is the platform's decision rather than yours or suteo's. Paid subscriptions, for example, sit with the platform's payment provider. That is checked against your account before any proposal, and the proposal says plainly what will move and what cannot.

## What is in scope

Usually included:

- the pages a writer or publication needs, such as home, books or work, the archive, about and contact;
- moving existing writing in;
- connecting your domain;
- a sign-up link or form for the newsletter service you already use;
- a short written guide to adding a new piece and to how the site is put together.

Not included, or quoted separately:

- shops, checkouts and paywalls (a site can link to a service that handles sales, as selador does);
- member accounts and logins;
- writing or editing the copy. The words are yours.

There is no fixed menu or price list. Each project is quoted in writing once its scope is clear.

## How an enquiry works

Everything happens in writing, so there is a record of what was agreed.

1. **You write to [hello@suteo.dev](mailto:hello@suteo.dev?subject=suteo%20website%20enquiry).**
2. **A written reply** says whether the work fits and asks whatever is still unclear.
3. **A written proposal** sets out the scope, what moves and what cannot, the price and the order of work. Nothing starts until you agree to it.
4. **The build** happens on a private preview address that you can read on your own devices.
5. **Launch and handover.** Your domain is pointed at the finished site, and the files and the written guide are yours.

A first message is most useful when it says:

- what you publish, and roughly how much of it there is;
- where it lives now, with a link;
- whether you already own your domain, and where it is registered;
- any date that matters, such as a book launch;
- two or three sites whose look you like.

Work is carried out in English.
